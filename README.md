# DataBridge

**Student-led data projects at the University of Alberta.**

DataBridge is a proposed interdisciplinary student club that helps students apply statistics, programming, and data skills to real-world questions. The club is in development and has not yet received official student group recognition.

**Co-Presidents:** Estella Li and Kaleb Pham.

## Our first project

### Which Edmonton neighbourhoods consistently have the most pothole reports?

We will compare neighbourhood reporting counts across several years and identify areas that repeatedly appear near the top.

**Status:** Planning. Initial source checks are complete; the final dataset, neighbourhood matching, and team review are still pending. We have no research findings yet.

## What we will do

1. Confirm usable Edmonton 311 data, aiming to compare **2023–2025**.
2. Keep road and alley pothole reports; exclude information-only inquiries.
3. Count reports for each neighbourhood in each year.
4. Compare each year's top 10 neighbourhoods and show how often each appears.
5. Summarize the findings in one short research report, using tables and a map where useful.

The date range and top-10 comparison are proposed choices to confirm before analysis. Reports reflect what people submit to 311; they do not count every physical pothole.

## Follow SCI 201's research process

| Phase | Our task |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Plan and prepare** | Agree on the question, read relevant sources, and check the data and research ethics. |
| **2. Collect data** | Obtain the records, clean them, and document missing information. |
| **3. Analyze and interpret** | Compare yearly neighbourhood counts and explain the patterns and limitations. |
| **4. Write and share** | Write the research report and choose how to share the findings. |

This framework follows the supplied SCI 201 Fall 2026 syllabus, pp. 1 and 5. DataBridge is an independent club project.

## Deliverables

**Main deliverable:** one short research report, written in **LaTeX** and exported as a PDF. Tables and maps belong inside the report.

**Optional ways to share the same findings:** a club website post, a research poster, or a simple dashboard. Choose what is useful; we do not need to produce all three.
